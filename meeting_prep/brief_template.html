1. At a glance

The thesis idea. Use model-agnostic feature effects and interactions (ALE, GADGET, Shapley-Taylor) as a precise specification of a behavior of TabPFN, then find the internal components that implement exactly that behavior, and show that intervening on them changes that behavior specifically.

Where the field stands

  • Work on TabPFN's internals picked up in 2026. Probing and neuron studies came first (Gupta et al. and Knauer & Rodner, January 2026). The first causal and mechanistic studies followed in May-June: Bilos et al. (what TabPFN computes: an attention vote at layer 9), Balef et al. (when: the answer is decodable by layer 5, late layers reformat it; ICML 2026) and Gupta et al. (which heads matter; ICML 2026 workshop).
  • Feature-effect work on TabPFN exists (Rundel et al. 2024; TabDistill, April 2026, for interactions), but stays at the behavior level and never looks inside.
  • We found no paper that connects the two: one that takes a specified feature effect or interaction and tests, with a null model, which components implement it. The general logic of "specify, then test against the internals" does exist in LLM research (causal abstraction), so the novelty is applying it with feature-effect estimands to tabular foundation models. This rests on our collection plus a targeted web search on 2026-10-19, not a systematic review (3.1).

Progress since the proposal

  • A first pilot ran on 2026-10-19 (section 4). The pipeline works and is validated on the real TabPFN v2 checkpoint.
  • Main lesson: TabPFN's interaction behavior changes by about half between two 256-row samples of the same task, so mechanism claims must be tested across many contexts.
  • Tentative: one selective attention set per placement, and its type flips with feature pairing. One context each, so not yet evidence.
  • New hypothesis (section 5): attention gathers X1 and X2 into one token, and an MLP computes their product. It targets the MLPs, which every paper finds important and none explains.

Changes to the proposal I'd suggest (from the review)

  1. Cite Balef et al. and reframe RQ2: given known depthwise redundancy, is an interaction localizable at all, and at what granularity?
  2. Give ΔB a null model (random component sets) and an estimator-noise floor. Implemented in the pilot; the floor needs splitting into estimator noise and context variation.
  3. Report target vs non-target ΔB as a selectivity ratio. Implemented in the pilot.

2. Papers discussed: main ideas

Citation counts are Semantic Scholar's (merged preprint + published versions). read in full means text, figures and appendix; partly read means abstract, introduction and key sections.

Mechanistic and behavioral analyses of TabPFN

Interpretable Machine Learning for TabPFN

Rundel, Kobialka, von Crailsheim, Feurer, Nagler, Rügamer (LMU) · xAI 2024 (CCIS) · citations · read in full
Main idea
Adapt standard IML methods (ICE/PD, ALE, Shapley values, LOCO, data valuation) to TabPFN. Because "refitting" TabPFN is a single forward pass with a different context, methods that need retraining become cheap and exact.
Key point
The value function matters: removing a feature by refitting explains the data; imputing it explains the model.
For the thesis
The behavioral side of the thesis builds on this. It explains what TabPFN does, never how.

What exactly has TabPFN learned to do?

McCarter · ICLR 2024 Blogpost Track (v2 appendix 2025) · citations · read in full
Main idea
Black-box probing: feed TabPFN tiny hand-built datasets and plot what it predicts.
Key findings
Confidence falls off with distance from the data; it behaves roughly like a nearest-neighbor/kernel smoother in 2D. With ensembling, TabPFN v2 learns parity to >99% accuracy from ~12% of the truth table, a function where every main and pairwise effect is flat.
For the thesis
Parity shows feature effects can specify nothing while the model is accurate: a stress case for "feature effects as specifications".
Caveat
Anecdotal (single configurations, no statistics); one model with ensembling solved parity, one without did not, unexplained.

A Mechanistic Study of Tabular Foundation Models

Bilos, Wilson, Schneider, Nevmyvaka (Morgan Stanley) · arXiv May 2026 · citations · read in full
Main idea
Find a simple "readout rule" that reproduces each model's predictions from its internals, test it causally, and transplant it across models (TabPFN v2, TabICL v2, Mitra; 49 datasets).
Key findings
TabPFN v2 predicts through an attention-weighted vote over context rows at layer 9, using a learned similarity. TabICL uses a nearest-class-prototype rule instead; swapping rules costs 33-40 points. Column-order sensitivity comes from a few positional parameters that can be zeroed for free. Toy models prove when order-blind architectures must fail.
For the thesis
The closest prior mechanistic work, and the obvious paper to compare with. Their toy analysis shows feature pairs are what let one layer represent XOR-type interactions.
Caveat
"Causal" evidence shows necessity only; the layer-wise probe looks flawed (below the majority-class baseline on early layers); attacks are not shown to be mechanism-specific. Ensembling not reported.

Is One Layer Enough? Understanding Inference Dynamics in Tabular Foundation Models

Balef, Koshil, Eggensperger (TU Dortmund, Tübingen) · ICML 2026 · citations · read in full
Main idea
Six LLM-interpretability tools (layer similarity, probes, per-layer retrained decoders, skip/repeat/swap ablations, self-repair) on six tabular foundation models.
Key findings
For TabPFN v2 the answer is decodable by about layer 5 of 12; the late layers mainly convert it into the output head's format. The first layer is irreplaceable; most other layers can be skipped and later layers repair the damage. A single layer looped 6 times matches 6 layers in a small TabPFN clone.
For the thesis
The biggest risk to RQ2: redundancy means single-component ablations can show no effect even for components that matter. Ablate sets, and use their query-only probe protocol.
Caveat
"Normalized performance" is never defined; looping saves parameters, not compute; toy scale only.

Where Computation Lives Inside TabPFN: Causal Localisation of Attention Head Function

Gupta, Kumar, Mandal, Deshpande (BITS Pilani) · ICML 2026 Workshop on Foundation Models for Structured Data · citations · partly read
Main idea
Activation patching, ablation and attention entropy on TabPFN-2.5's feature-wise attention heads, on two synthetic regression tasks.
Key findings
One head is 2-5x more causally important than the others at its peak layer, and that layer shifts with task complexity. Contrastive steering vectors do not transfer across samples.
For the thesis
Closest in method (patching on synthetic tasks); the steering result is partial prior evidence for RQ3 (mechanisms may be context-specific).

TabPFN Through The Looking Glass

Gupta, Sethi, Kumar (BITS Pilani) · NeurIPS 2026 Workshop XAI4Science (poster); arXiv Jan 2026 · citations · abstract and introduction
Main idea
Probe TabPFN's layers for intermediate quantities: linear-regression coefficients, intermediate values of compound expressions, and the final answer.
For the thesis
Probing for an intermediate quantity is the template for testing whether, say, a product X1X2 is represented. Correlational only.

Theory and tools

Statistical Foundations of Prior-Data Fitted Networks

Nagler (LMU) · ICML 2023 · citations · key claims
Main idea
A frequentist account: a PFN is a pre-tuned but untrained predictor. Its variance vanishes when its sensitivity to single training samples vanishes; its bias vanishes only if it is localized around the test point, which the architecture does not guarantee.
For the thesis
Makes a mechanistic prediction (localization) that Bilos's similarity vote can be tested against. Nagler co-authored two papers the proposal cites, so its absence would be noticed.

Bayes' Power for Explaining In-Context Learning Generalizations

Müller, Hollmann, Hutter · arXiv 2024 · citations · key claims
Main idea
The opposite reading: the Bayesian posterior view, not maximum likelihood, explains how in-context learners generalize. Written by the TabPFN authors.
For the thesis
With Nagler, a live theory dispute with no mechanistic evidence on either side; a framing sentence for the thesis's motivation.

nanoTabPFN: A Lightweight and Educational Reimplementation of TabPFN

Pfefferle, Hog, Purucker, Hutter · EurIPS 2025 Workshop "AI for Tabular Data" · citations · key sections
Main idea
TabPFN v2's architecture in under 500 lines, with a training loop on pre-generated prior data. In a small-data setting it trains in about a minute on one GPU.
For the thesis
Makes the pretraining prior an experimental variable: pretrain with and without interactions and check whether the localized mechanism appears only in the first. Budget: Balef et al. needed 12-69 A100-hours per research-scale nanoTabPFN model.

Accurate predictions on small data with a tabular foundation model (TabPFN v2)

Hollmann et al. · Nature 2025 · citations · context only
Role
The model under study: per-cell tokens, attention alternating across rows and across features, pretrained on synthetic structural-causal-model data. Shown for scale, not as a realistic citation benchmark.

4. First pilot experiment (2026-10-19)

A first test of openings 1 and 2 above, built and run on 2026-10-19. Code: fe_mech_pipeline/. Full report: pilot_report.html; its section 0 explains the results in plain words. Design choices are recorded in DECISIONS.md #9-#18.

What was built

  • Task: the label depends on X1·X2 (switched on when X3 > 0), plus main effects of X1 and X2 and one noise feature.
  • Behavioral specification: ALE main effects and the second-order ALE interaction surface, implemented in numpy. It recovers known functions to within about 1%.
  • Model: the original TabPFN v2 classifier checkpoint (7,244,554 parameters, matching Balef et al.), with one ensemble member and preprocessing off. Note: tabpfn 8.2.0's own "v2" default is a different, fine-tuned checkpoint. Hooks were verified to leave outputs bitwise identical when inactive.
  • Interventions: mean ablation of all feature-attention heads, all item-attention heads, or the MLP, one layer at a time (36 sets), with zero ablation as a contrast.
  • Evidence standard from the review: a random-component null (25 sets per type), a noise floor, and selectivity against the X2 main effect. Two hypotheses were pre-registered before any result existed: H1, the interaction is carried by feature attention; H2, the answer depends on whether X1 and X2 share a feature pair.
  • Scale: one 256-row context per placement, 293 forward passes, 13 minutes on the laptop CPU.

What changes for the thesis

  1. Move context generalization (RQ3) into the core: test every claim across 10 or more independent contexts.
  2. Split the noise floor, and pre-register the corrected rules before the next run.
  3. Choose and justify the replacement: mean, zero, or patching in activations from an interaction-free context. Patching also gives the causal-abstraction framing from section 3.1.
  4. Replace the MLP null with one that matches the candidates' structure.
  5. Add a baseline learner (XGBoost or a GAM) to show whether TabPFN's context variability is unusual.
Is it a paper yet? Not yet; this is a feasibility pilot.
  • Workshop paper: realistic after the multi-context run with these fixes, e.g. ICML 2027 workshops (around late April 2027, estimated). A clean methodology paper with an honest result fits there, even a negative one.
  • Main track: also needs several tasks and interaction strengths, causal validation beyond ablation, a second model or the nanoTabPFN prior ablation, and clear positioning against Bilos, Balef and causal abstraction. ICLR 2028 (around late September 2027) is the realistic target.

5. New idea: what do TabPFN's MLPs store? "Attention gathers, MLP computes"

untested hypothesis Everything in this section is my proposal, not a result from any paper or from the pilot.

Why the MLPs deserve attention

The published explanations of TabPFN are all about attention. MLPs keep showing up as important, but nobody explains them:

SourceWhat it found about MLPs
Bilos et al.Skipping block 0's MLP drops the layer-9 probe by 37.6 points ("sets up the coordinate frame"); the MLPs in blocks 5-6 are the next most critical; knocking out MLPs collapses accuracy (0.87 to 0.42); on 10-class problems the late MLPs (layers 9-11) carry what the attention vote misses. Their own summary: layer-9 attention is "necessary but not sufficient".
Balef et al.Ablate whole layers (attention + MLP together), so they never separate the two.
Gupta et al.Study attention heads only.
Our pilotAblating a whole MLP layer changed the interaction a lot but the main effects just as much (e.g. layer 7: 0.84 vs 0.89). It looks like general machinery, but whole-layer ablation is a sledgehammer.

The LLM analogy, and where it breaks

In language models, MLPs act like key-value memories (Geva et al., 2021): one weight matrix detects a pattern ("Eiffel Tower"), the other writes an output ("Paris"). Factual recall has been located in mid-layer MLPs and edited there (Meng et al., 2022, ROME). Those facts are knowledge memorized in training and not present in the input.

TabPFN v2 was pretrained on synthetic data, so there are no world facts to memorize, and everything about a task is in the context. Its stored knowledge can only be what it learned from the prior: operations and inductive biases. (The one exception is Real-TabPFN, which continued pretraining on real datasets; it could memorize real facts or even benchmark datasets, a side question about contamination.)

What TabPFN's MLPs could store

A key architectural fact: TabPFN v2 makes every table cell its own token, and the MLP is applied to each cell token separately. Only attention moves information between cells. So an MLP can only transform what is already inside one token. Three hypotheses follow:

  1. A library of nonlinear operations (learned feature engineering): squares, thresholds, bump shapes, saturating curves. The synthetic prior generates many such nonlinearities, so storing them in MLPs would make sense.
  2. Computing interactions ("attention gathers, MLP computes"): feature attention brings X1 and X2 into the same token, and the following MLP computes their nonlinear combination, such as X1·X2. One caveat: attention scores are themselves products of two tokens, so attention can express some interaction through which rows it looks at. But writing a product into the representation most naturally needs the MLP's nonlinearity. Bilos's toy models point the same way: XOR "requires at least one nonlinear interaction".
  3. Format conversion and calibration late in the network: Balef's last stages (convert to the output head's format, calibrate confidence) happen in the final layers, and in regression (an output distribution over about 5,000 bins) the final layers matter in both papers. Late MLPs could store "shape templates" for predictive distributions.

Why this is a good fit for the thesis. It refines RQ2 into a sharp, falsifiable claim ("attention gathers, MLP computes"), targets exactly the part the existing papers leave unexplained, and experiment 1 runs on the pilot pipeline almost unchanged. It also links the feature-effect specification (an interaction surface) to a concrete mechanism type (a product computed in an MLP).

8. Questions for the meeting
  1. Model and version. The pilot used the original TabPFN v2 checkpoint that Bilos and Balef studied (12 layers, 7.24M parameters). Keep it as the main model, with nanoTabPFN for the prior ablation? The current tabpfn package defaults to v3, and v2.5/v2.6 have licenses that complicate analysis.
  2. Behavioral specification. ALE + GADGET, or Shapley-Taylor interactions, as the primary specification? (The pilot used ALE.)
  3. RQ2 framing. Accept the reframing around depthwise redundancy (Balef)?
  4. Evidence standard. Adopt the random-component null, the noise floor and the selectivity ratio as the headline metric, with the floor split into estimator noise and context variation (section 4)?
  5. Context generalization. Given the pilot's main lesson, should RQ3 move into the minimum viable thesis?
  6. Ablation replacement. Mean, zero, or patching from an interaction-free context? Mean and zero ablation disagreed in the pilot (rank correlation 0.28).
  7. Compute. RunPod for the multi-context run (likely under an hour of GPU) and later the nanoTabPFN prior ablation (hours to days)? Is there a budget?
  8. MLP hypothesis. Make "attention gathers, MLP computes" (section 5) the sharpened RQ2, with the before/after-MLP probe as the next cheap experiment?
  9. Publication target. Which venue and deadline to aim for (section 7), and whether to go workshop-first (section 4 has my assessment).
  10. Scope check. Is "one specified interaction, one model, one localization and one validation method" the agreed minimum viable thesis?
  11. Positioning. Should the thesis be framed explicitly as causal abstraction with an IML estimand as the high-level model (section 3.1)? And who runs the systematic novelty search before the proposal is finalized?
